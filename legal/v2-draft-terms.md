## 2. What jipbap is

You tell jipbap what is in your kitchen. It tells you which recipes you can make
from that, which you are one or two items short of, and what to buy to close the
gap. It keeps a grocery list and a record of what you cooked.

**It is a finding tool, not a cookbook.** For every recipe in it, the method
lives on the site that published it and that is where we send you.

## 3. The recipes are not ours

Recipes in jipbap were published by other people — food writers, bloggers, and
cooks who own what they wrote.

- We show a **title, a source, an ingredient list, and a link**. We do not
  reproduce the method, and we do not present anyone's work as ours.
- The link goes to the original. When you cook, you cook from their page, and
  their site gets the visit.
- Ingredient lists are read by a parser. **It makes mistakes.** Recipes it could
  not read completely are held back rather than shown wrong, which is why the
  library is smaller than the number of recipes we have.
- If you published a recipe and want it out of jipbap, write to
  **hello@jipbap.com** and we will remove it. We will not ask you to justify
  the request.

## 5. What jipbap does not promise

This is the section that matters, so it is in plain words.

### 5.1 The dietary and allergen filters are not a safety check

jipbap can hide recipes containing things you have excluded, and it can flag
allergens it knows about. **Do not rely on this if the consequence of an error
is a medical one.**

Three honest reasons, all of them structural:

- Our ingredient data is machine-assigned and only partly reviewed. It has
  errors, and we know it has errors.
- An ingredient list is not a label. "Gochujang" does not tell us whether that
  jar contains wheat; the jar in your hand does.
- Cross-contamination, shared equipment and reformulated products are invisible
  to anything we can see.

**If an ingredient can hurt you, read the packet.** Our filter is a convenience
for narrowing a list, not a clearance to eat something.

### 5.2 "You have everything" is a claim about your pantry, not your kitchen

jipbap knows what you told it you have. It does not know the jar is empty, that
the scallions turned, or that the packet is three months past its date. Cooking
from what it says is right is still cooking from what **you** said is right.
